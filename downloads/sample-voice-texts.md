# Sample voice texts (My Voice side quest)

Illustrative texts for training purposes, written for this workshop. Use these if you'd rather not paste your own writing. They share one recognisable voice: short sentences, direct, warm, a question at the end, numbers rounded and always explained.

## 1. Email to a leadership team

Subject: Where we stand after Q3

Team,

Short version: we grew faster than planned and earned less than planned. Both are true, and we should say both out loud.

The storm in August cost us more than any single month this year. That one will pass. Rising repair costs in motor will not pass on their own, so pricing moves in January.

What I need from you this week: one example from your area where a faster claim made a customer stay with us. I want to take real stories into the board, not just ratios.

Thanks for carrying a heavy quarter. What would make the next storm easier for your team?

Anna

## 2. Internal memo

To: Claims and Operations leads
Re: Lessons from the storm weeks

We handled more claims in three weeks than in a normal quarter. People worked weekends. Customers still waited too long.

Three things worked: the hotline triage, the temporary photo upload, and daily stand-ups across teams. One thing didn't: manual intake. It became the bottleneck within 48 hours.

So the question isn't whether we automate intake. It's how quickly we can do it safely, with a person still deciding every payment.

I'll bring a proposal to the board next week. Tell me by Friday what must not get lost.

## 3. LinkedIn-style post

Three weeks in August taught me more about our company than any strategy offsite.

When the storm hit, our claims teams didn't wait for a plan. They built one on the fly: a hotline triage, photo uploads, stand-ups at 7:30 every morning.

What slowed us down wasn't people. It was paperwork.

That's the honest case for automation in insurance: not fewer people, but people spending their time where customers need them most.

What's the one process in your organisation that only breaks when it matters?
