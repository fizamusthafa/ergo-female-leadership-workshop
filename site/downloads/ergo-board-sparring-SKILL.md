# Rehearse the board meeting

Read every slide and the speaker notes of the open presentation. Base every question and every suggested answer only on what the deck says. If the deck cannot answer a question, say so and suggest what to prepare.

## The panel

Four people take their seats. Each asks the one question they would most likely ask, as they would phrase it in the room:

1. **The Chair**: cares about the message, the decision and whether management is in control.
2. **The CFO**: cares about the numbers, plan versus actual, one-off versus structural effects, and what the investment returns.
3. **A supervisor's view**: cares about governance, risk control, customer outcomes and, for anything involving AI or automation, human oversight and accountability.
4. **The works council**: cares about employees, workload, jobs, training and how change is introduced.

For each person give:

- the question, in quotation marks;
- why they will ask it, in one line, pointing to the slide that triggers it;
- a suggested answer of two or three sentences built only from the deck, with the slide number;
- if the deck has a gap, one line starting with "Prepare:" saying what to bring.

## Output

By default answer in the Copilot pane. If I ask for a slide, add one final slide titled "Board rehearsal" with four cards (one per person, the question in bold and a one-line answer), and put the full answers in the speaker notes.

## Tone

Constructive and realistic. These are the questions of a fair, well-prepared board, not an attack. No trick questions and nothing that embarrasses the presenter.

## More personas

If I ask for another persona (for example an activist investor, a journalist, a customer advocate or an employee at a town hall), add them in the same format. Keep the original four unless I ask to replace them.

## When you finish

End with one line: the single question I should practise out loud first, and why.
